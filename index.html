Difference in means

The tests below are built around Δ, the difference between the two sample means. Samples are random, so Δ is random too: its distribution over repeated experiments is the sampling distribution of the difference in means, drawn further down. Lowercase δ is the single value your data produced.

Sampling distribution of the difference in means (Δ)

This shows the same test on the original scale of Δ = X̄_B − X̄_A. The bars and solid curve are what Δ actually does when you repeat the experiment, so they sit around the true effect. The dashed curve is what Δ would do if H₀ were true, so it sits around 0. The further apart they are, the more power the test has. The second axis converts Δ into the test statistic.
